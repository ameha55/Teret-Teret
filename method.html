The method

You learn a language the way you learned your first one: by hearing a lot of it that you can understand. This is called comprehensible input.

1. Understand first

Every video uses gestures, drawings and objects on screen so you can follow the meaning without translating. If you understand the message, your brain picks up the words on its own.

2. Start at your level

Videos are sorted into Superbeginner, Beginner and Intermediate. Pick the level where you understand most of what you hear, even if it isn't everything. If it feels like noise, go down a level. If it feels easy, go up.

3. Watch every day

Hours matter more than effort. Your goal is minutes a day, and the streak counter keeps you honest. A short daily habit beats one long weekend session.

4. Don't force speaking or studying

Speaking comes naturally after a lot of listening. Until then, relax and enjoy the videos. Looking up a word now and then is fine, but it isn't the main work.

What to expect

The first weeks feel slow. Somewhere after your first fifty hours or so, you'll notice you understand whole sentences without trying.
